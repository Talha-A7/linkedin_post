## 2. The strategies that work

### 1. A specific, dated, personal story beats advice (both creators, every top post)
Every post above 1.2× includes **an age, a year, a dollar amount or a named place** plus a real cost. Examples: fired 3× before 28, a $70M org in late 2018, sleeping on a couch, a first $10k from free work. The bottom posts by the same creators are general lectures ("smart people procrastinate", "lifestyle creep", 0.63–0.65×).

### 2. Line 1 is a number paradox or a contrarian claim
The top hooks pair a big credible number with something unexpected: $15M profit *by refusing to grow*; 880k followers *but 30 minutes a day*; 10 years for a job *vs* 10 weeks for a business; "never work for free" is *bad* advice. None of the top 15 opens with a question.

### 3. A small, memorable framework near the end
3 questions to vet advice, 3 rules for impatience, a 5-step causal loop, a 4-3-2-1 content rule. Numbered lists of 3–5 with one line each are easy to save and repost.

### 4. Short paragraphs, white space, 900–1,800 characters
Both creators use 1–2 sentence paragraphs with blank lines. Story posts run long (Creator A's top posts are ~1,200–1,800 characters); people still read to the end because each line advances the story.

### 5. One consistent call to action, after the value
Creator A ends **every** post with the same free 30-page guide. Creator B rotates a lead magnet, "book a call", and **"Repost ♻️ if…"** plus a **P.S. question**. The CTA always comes *after* the story or lesson.

### 6. Promotion-first posts underperform badly
Same creators, same audience: the webinar promo (0.31×), the sales pitch (0.39×) and the sponsored post (0.67×) are the three worst. Selling works as the **last line of a story**, not as the post.

### 7. Participation mechanics drive comments (Creator B)
A P.S. question readers can answer about themselves ("where do you want to work?", "what's your 2026 commitment?"), invitations to tag someone, and open applications. Her community posts get ~1,100 comments on ~2,400 reactions.

### 8. Visuals carry some of the biggest posts
Creator B's photo milestone (1.82×) and a video with almost no text (2.1M views) rank near the top. Creator A is text-only, so the visuals are a lever, not a requirement.

### 9. Volume and consistency
Creator A posts **daily** (19 posts in ~2 weeks); Creator B posts about **4×/week** (she shares a "4 posts, 3 content types, 2 audiences, 1 lead magnet" rule). One topic, told 100 ways. Small accounts in the sample post sporadically and on random topics.

## 3. What does NOT explain the likes

- **Links in the post:** Creator A puts a link in the body of every post and still gets thousands of reactions. For very large accounts the "links kill reach" rule is weaker than commonly claimed. For small accounts, keep links in the first comment until you can test it.
- **Hashtags:** the top posts use 0–1. The low-performing small-account posts use 4–8.
- **Polish:** both top creators break a few "AI-tell" rules our app enforces (stacked threes, "read that last line again", lots of one-line paragraphs). Their established voice and audience absorb it. A new account using the same tics tends to read as generic.

---

## 4. What this means for you

1. **Pick one topic and stick to it.** For example, building PairVybe or Tectsoft's work. Tell it as dated stories, lessons and occasional offers.
2. **Write from real moments with numbers:** your own 10/100 profile score, launch dates, user counts, costs, mistakes. That's exactly what the top posts are made of.
3. **Lead with a number paradox or a contrarian claim you can prove.**
4. **Finish with one small framework, one P.S. question, and one CTA.** Never make the CTA the whole post.
5. **Post 3–4×/week for at least 90 days** before judging. The 0.5%-of-followers rate means your first posts will be small in absolute terms; that's normal.
6. **Add a real photo** (you at work, a screenshot of a result) to the posts that matter most.
